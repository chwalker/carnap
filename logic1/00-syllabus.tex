In this course, we will work entirely from these course notes and the lectures
based on them. That said, diverse presentations sometimes make the material
easier to understand. And introductory logic has nothing if not diverse
presentations. There are three that are worth mentioning, two of which served
as major sources for these course notes.

\begin{outline}[enumerate]

\1 The Open Logic textbook \emph{Forall x} formed the backbone of structure for
the course:
\begin{quote}
  \fullcite{Mag25}
\end{quote}
Although these notes are not a fork of their project, they benefitted a great
deal from some of its resources. Most notably, a LaTex style package for
Fitch-style proof systems.

\1 The \emph{Logic Matters} introduction to formal logic is a fantastic resource
for deeper dives and a more philosophical treatment of the material.
\begin{quote}
  \fullcite{Smi20}
\end{quote}

\1 Finally, the \emph{LTF Gamut} series was my own introduction to formal logic.
\begin{quote}
  \fullcite{Gam90} \\ \\
  \fullcite{Gam91}
\end{quote}
It offers a highly accessible introduction to the material with a focus on its
application to the study of ordinary language.

\end{outline}

I have drawn examples and exercises from all three. For \cite{Smi20} and
\cite{Mag25} I have included page numbers for the portions most closely aligned
with each lecture. Both books are free to download and can be purchased online
for about \$10.
